If you've never had your vehicle inspected in Pennsylvania before—or it's just been a while—you might be wondering what you actually need to bring with you.

The good news is that a PA state inspection doesn't require a lot of paperwork. But there are a few things you'll want to have ready before you pull in.

### What You Need to Bring

Pennsylvania keeps the requirements straightforward. When you arrive for your [state inspection](/services#state-inspections-emissions), you'll need:

* **Your vehicle.** The inspection is performed on the car itself, so it needs to be drivable and brought in person.
* **Your current vehicle registration.** Inspectors are required to verify that the registration matches the vehicle being inspected. Make sure it hasn't expired.
* **Proof of insurance.** A valid insurance ID card is required before work can begin. A digital version on your phone is perfectly fine.

### What About Emissions?

In Bucks County, most vehicles are subject to both a **safety inspection** and an **emissions inspection**, and both are typically performed at the same visit. There's no extra paperwork for the emissions portion—the technician simply connects a scanner to your vehicle's OBD-II port to check your onboard computer for any active faults.

Two things are worth knowing before you come in:

* **If your [check engine light](/blog/what-to-do-when-your-check-engine-light-comes-on) is on,** it's best to have it [looked at first](/services#check-engine-diagnostics). For vehicles subject to emissions testing, an illuminated check engine light will typically prevent the vehicle from passing. (We cover why in detail in our post on [whether your car will pass](/blog/will-my-car-pass-pa-state-inspection-newtown).)
* **Low-mileage vehicles may qualify for an emissions exemption.** If your vehicle has been driven very little since your last inspection, give us a call before booking—we can check whether it qualifies and let you know the earliest date you'd be able to schedule.

### Is There Anything Else That Can Cause a Failure?

Yes. The safety portion of a PA inspection covers a range of components beyond just your engine. Inspectors check things like:

* Brakes and brake lines
* Tires (tread depth and condition)
* Lights (headlights, brake lights, turn signals)
* Horn
* Windshield wipers and washers
* Steering and suspension components
* Exhaust system
* Seatbelts and mirrors

If any of these components don't meet PennDOT standards, the vehicle will receive a rejection sticker rather than a passing sticker. You'll then have a set period of time to have the issues repaired and return for a re-inspection.

If you're unsure whether your vehicle will pass, it can be worth scheduling a general [vehicle inspection](/services#state-inspections-emissions) beforehand to catch any potential problems ahead of time.

### When Does My Vehicle Need to Be Inspected?

Pennsylvania requires annual safety and emissions inspections. Your current inspection sticker shows the expiration month and year. Vehicles can be inspected up to 90 days before the sticker expires without losing time on the new sticker—so there's no penalty for getting it done a little early.

Driving with an expired inspection sticker in Pennsylvania can result in a traffic citation, so it's worth staying on top of the expiration date.
