Newtown sits just a few miles from the New Jersey border, and plenty of drivers in Bucks County cross between the two states regularly—for work, errands, or the commute down I-295. If you've ever wondered whether your PA inspection is valid in NJ, or what actually differs between the two states' requirements, here's a clear breakdown.

### The Short Answer

Your vehicle registration is issued by one state, and that's the state whose inspection rules apply to you. If your car is registered in Pennsylvania, you need a PA inspection—regardless of how often you drive in New Jersey, and vice versa.

### Pennsylvania State Inspection

Pennsylvania requires **annual** inspections for most registered vehicles. The inspection has two components:

**Safety inspection** — a physical check of brakes, tires, lights, steering, suspension, exhaust, wipers, horn, seatbelts, and mirrors. This is performed visually and mechanically by a licensed inspector.

**Emissions inspection** — an OBD-II scan of your vehicle's onboard computer to check for active fault codes and confirm that emissions-related systems are functioning correctly.

Both are typically done during the same appointment. In Bucks County, emissions testing is required for most vehicles. Passing both earns you an annual sticker that goes on your windshield.

If a vehicle fails either portion, it fails testing. The owner then has a set period to make repairs and return for re-inspection and a sticker.

For more detail on what PA inspectors check during the safety portion, see our post on [how to know if your car will pass PA state inspection](/blog/will-my-car-pass-pa-state-inspection-newtown).

### New Jersey State Inspection

New Jersey handles things a bit differently.

**NJ does not have a separate safety inspection for most passenger vehicles.** Since 2010, the state eliminated the safety inspection component for most private passenger cars and light trucks. New Jersey's inspection program focuses almost entirely on **emissions**.

However, there are exceptions:

* **Commercial vehicles** are still subject to safety inspections in NJ.
* **Vehicles that have been in accidents** may require a safety inspection before returning to the road.
* **Certain model years** may be exempt from emissions testing altogether, depending on the vehicle's age.

New Jersey inspection frequency depends on the vehicle:

* New vehicles are typically exempt for the first few years.
* Most other vehicles require inspection **every two years**.

New Jersey uses both OBD-II scanning (similar to PA) and a tailpipe emissions test for older vehicles that predate OBD-II systems.

### Key Differences at a Glance

* **Safety inspection:** Required annually in PA. Largely eliminated for private passenger vehicles in NJ.
* **Emissions inspection:** Required in both states for most vehicles. NJ may also require a tailpipe test for older vehicles.
* **Frequency:** Annual in PA. Every two years in NJ for most vehicles.
* **Sticker:** PA uses a windshield sticker. NJ inspection results are recorded electronically; there is no physical sticker requirement.

### What If I Just Moved?

If you've recently moved from New Jersey to Pennsylvania—or the other way around—you'll need to re-register your vehicle in your new state and get it inspected under that state's program.

Pennsylvania requires new residents to register their vehicle within 20 days of establishing residency. Once registered in PA, you'll be subject to PA's annual inspection requirements going forward.
