One of the most common questions we hear at Burns Auto Repair is: **"How often should I change my oil?"**

The answer isn't as simple as it used to be.

Years ago, most vehicles followed the traditional "every 3,000 miles" rule. Modern engines, improved oil formulations, and manufacturer recommendations have changed that. Depending on your vehicle and driving habits, you may be able to go much longer between oil changes.

For drivers in Newtown, Yardley, and throughout Bucks County, understanding the right oil change interval can help extend engine life, improve fuel economy, and prevent costly repairs.

### Why Engine Oil Is So Important

Motor oil does much more than simply lubricate engine components.

It helps:

* Reduce friction between moving parts
* Remove heat from critical engine components
* Prevent sludge and deposits from forming
* Protect against corrosion
* Keep internal engine parts clean

Over time, oil breaks down and becomes contaminated with dirt, fuel residue, and combustion byproducts. When that happens, it becomes less effective at protecting your engine.

Regular [scheduled maintenance](/services#scheduled-maintenance) helps ensure your engine stays properly protected.

### How Often Should You Change Your Oil?

The best place to start is your vehicle owner's manual.

Many modern vehicles recommend oil changes somewhere between:

* 5,000 to 7,500 miles for normal driving
* 3,000 to 5,000 miles for severe driving conditions
* Up to 10,000 miles or more for some vehicles using synthetic oil

However, mileage isn't the only factor.

Your driving habits can significantly impact how quickly oil deteriorates.

### What Counts as Severe Driving?

Many drivers are surprised to learn they fall into the "severe service" category.

Examples include:

* Frequent short trips
* Stop-and-go traffic
* Extended idling
* Towing or hauling heavy loads
* Driving in extremely hot or cold temperatures
* Regular city driving

If you regularly drive around Newtown, Yardley, Langhorne, or other busy Bucks County roads where traffic is common, your vehicle may benefit from more frequent oil changes.

### Signs You May Be Due for an Oil Change

Even if you haven't reached your recommended mileage interval, your vehicle may show signs that it's time for service.

Watch for:

* Oil change or maintenance reminder lights
* Dark or dirty oil
* Increased engine noise
* Reduced fuel economy
* Rough engine performance
* Oil smell inside the vehicle

If you notice any of these symptoms, it's a good idea to schedule an inspection.

### Synthetic vs. Conventional Oil

Many newer vehicles require synthetic oil, while others can use either conventional or synthetic blends.

Synthetic oil generally offers:

* Better protection against engine wear
* Improved performance in extreme temperatures
* Longer service intervals
* Better resistance to breakdown

Using the correct oil specification recommended by your vehicle manufacturer is just as important as changing it regularly.

### What Happens If You Wait Too Long?

Delaying oil changes may save a little money in the short term, but it can become very expensive over time.

Old oil can lead to:

* Increased engine wear
* Sludge buildup
* Reduced fuel efficiency
* Overheating
* Premature engine failure

In severe cases, neglected oil maintenance can lead to major engine repairs or replacement.

Routine preventive maintenance is almost always less expensive than repairing damage caused by missed service intervals.

### Oil Changes Are About More Than Just Oil

When your vehicle comes in for an oil change, it's also an opportunity to identify small issues before they become major problems.

During service, technicians can inspect:

* Fluid levels
* Belts and hoses
* Tire condition
* Battery health
* Brake components
* Air filters

These inspections often catch maintenance concerns early, helping drivers avoid unexpected breakdowns.
